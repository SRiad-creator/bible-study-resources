The Story of Job

Job in History

Job 1:1–5

The Man Job

A blameless and upright man from the land of Uz, who feared God and shunned evil. The greatest of all the people of the East — blessed with seven sons, three daughters, and vast herds.

c. Patriarchal Era

Job 1:6–2:10

The Heavenly Court

The sons of God present themselves before the LORD, and Satan among them. God permits Satan to test Job — first his possessions, then his health. Job refuses to curse God: "The LORD gave, and the LORD has taken away; blessed be the name of the LORD."

The Divine Wager

Job 3–31

Job's Trial & the Great Debate

Three rounds of speeches between Job and his friends Eliphaz, Bildad, and Zophar. The friends insist suffering proves guilt; Job maintains his innocence and demands an audience with God. In the midst of agony, Job declares: "I know that my Redeemer lives."

The Dialogue Cycles

Job 32–37

Elihu Intervenes

A young man named Elihu, who has waited in silence, now speaks with passionate conviction. He rebukes both Job and his friends, offering a new perspective: suffering can be disciplinary and redemptive, not merely punitive. He points toward God's imminent self-revelation.

The Fourth Voice

Job 38–41

God Answers from the Whirlwind

"Where were you when I laid the foundations of the earth?" God speaks — not with explanations, but with overwhelming questions about creation, the cosmos, and the mighty creatures Behemoth and Leviathan. The answer to suffering is not information but divine encounter.

The Divine Answer

Job 42

Repentance & Double Restoration

"I have heard of You by the hearing of the ear, but now my eye sees You." Job repents, intercedes for his friends, and God restores him double — 14,000 sheep, 6,000 camels, seven sons, three daughters. He lives 140 more years and sees four generations.

Restoration & Blessing
Chapters 1-2

The Prologue — The Righteous Man & the Heavenly Wager

Historical
Moral
Patristic
  • Job's character: "blameless, upright, one who feared God and shunned evil" (1:1).
  • His wealth: 7,000 sheep, 3,000 camels, 500 yoke of oxen, 7 sons and 3 daughters.
  • The Heavenly Court scene — "the sons of God came to present themselves before the LORD, and Satan also came among them."
  • Satan's challenge: "Does Job fear God for nothing?" — the deepest question of the book.
  • First test: loss of wealth and children. Job's response: "The LORD gave, and the LORD has taken away; blessed be the name of the LORD" (1:21).
  • Second test: painful boils from head to foot. Job's wife: "Curse God and die!" Job: "Shall we indeed accept good from God, and shall we not accept adversity?" (2:10).
  • The three friends arrive — Eliphaz, Bildad, Zophar — sit in silence for 7 days.

Chapters 3-14

The First Cycle — Job's Lament & the Friends' Theology

Moral
Patristic
Soteriological
  • Job's lament (ch 3): curses the day of his birth — "Why did I not die at birth?"
  • Eliphaz (ch 4-5): appeals to experience — "Who ever perished being innocent?" Claims a night vision revealed truth.
  • Job's response (ch 6-7): "O that my grief were fully weighed!" — life is like forced labor.
  • Bildad (ch 8): appeals to tradition — "Does God pervert justice?"
  • Job's response (ch 9-10): "Though I were righteous, I could not answer Him" — desires a mediator.
  • Zophar (ch 11): appeals to divine wisdom — "Can you search out the deep things of God?"
  • Job's response (ch 12-14): "What you know, I also know" — yearns for his day in court with God. The famous cry: "If a man dies, shall he live again?" (14:14).
"May the day perish on which I was born, And the night in which it was said, 'A male child is conceived.'"
- Job 3:3
"Nor is there any mediator between us, Who may lay his hand on us both."
- Job 9:33
"If a man dies, shall he live again? All the days of my hard service I will wait, Till my change comes."
- Job 14:14
Chapters 15-21

The Second Cycle — Intensifying the Debate

Moral
Eschatological
Patristic
  • Friends become harsher. Eliphaz accuses Job of empty knowledge.
  • Job's greatest confession: "I know that my Redeemer lives, and He shall stand at last on the earth; and after my skin is destroyed, this I know, that in my flesh I shall see God" (19:25-26) — the most Christological verse in Job.
  • Job questions why the wicked prosper.

Chapters 22-31

The Third Cycle & Job's Final Defence

Moral
Christological
Patristic
  • Eliphaz makes direct false accusations against Job.
  • Job's Hymn to Wisdom (ch 28): "Where can wisdom be found?" — one of the most beautiful poems in Scripture. "The fear of the Lord, that is wisdom" (28:28).
  • Job's oath of innocence (ch 29-31): a majestic self-defence listing his righteous deeds — caring for the poor, widows, orphans, strangers.
  • Job challenges God directly: "Oh, that I had one to hear me! Here is my mark. Oh, that the Almighty would answer me!"

Chapters 32-37

Elihu's Speeches — The Fourth Voice

Patristic
Moral
Ecclesiological
  • Elihu the Buzite, young and angry, has waited his turn.
  • He rebukes both Job and the three friends.
  • His unique contribution: suffering can be disciplinary and redemptive, not only punitive.
  • "God speaks once, yes twice, yet man does not perceive it — in a dream, in a vision of the night" (33:14-15).
  • Points toward God speaking in nature and through suffering.
  • Prepares the way for God's own speech.
  • Some Fathers see Elihu as a type of the prophet preparing the way.
"For God may speak in one way, or in another, Yet man does not perceive it. In a dream, in a vision of the night, When deep sleep falls upon men, While slumbering on their beds,"
- Job 33:14-15
"Behold, God is exalted by His power; Who teaches like Him?"
- Job 36:22
Chapters 38-41

God Speaks from the Whirlwind — The Divine Answer

Christological
Eschatological
Patristic
  • THE climactic moment of the book. God finally answers — not with explanations but with questions.
  • "Who is this who darkens counsel by words without knowledge? Now prepare yourself like a man; I will question you, and you shall answer Me."
  • God's questions about creation: "Where were you when I laid the foundations of the earth?" (38:4).
  • The morning stars singing together (38:7), the storehouses of snow and hail, the constellations.
  • Behemoth (ch 40) and Leviathan (ch 41) — God's mastery over the mightiest creatures.
  • The Fathers interpret Leviathan as Satan himself, conquered only by God.
  • God never explains Job's suffering — He reveals HIMSELF. The answer to suffering is not information but encounter.
"Where were you when I laid the foundations of the earth? Tell Me, if you have understanding."
- Job 38:4
"When the morning stars sang together, And all the sons of God shouted for joy?"
- Job 38:7
"Look now at the behemoth, which I made along with you; He eats grass like an ox. See now, his strength is in his hips, And his power is in his stomach muscles."
- Job 40:15-16
"Can you draw out Leviathan with a hook, Or snare his tongue with a line which you lower?"
- Job 41:1
Chapter 42

The Epilogue — Repentance, Intercession & Double Restoration

Christological
Soteriological
Patristic
  • Job's repentance: "I have heard of You by the hearing of the ear, but now my eye sees You. Therefore I abhor myself, and repent in dust and ashes" (42:5-6).
  • God rebukes the three friends: "You have not spoken of Me what is right, as My servant Job has."
  • Job must PRAY for his friends — the sufferer becomes the intercessor (a type of Christ).
  • God restores Job DOUBLE: 14,000 sheep, 6,000 camels, 1,000 yoke of oxen. 7 sons and 3 daughters — whose names are given (Jemimah, Keziah, Keren-Happuch).
  • Job lives 140 more years and sees four generations.
☦ Coptic Orthodox Note: Job is read extensively during Holy Week; he is a type of Christ who suffers innocently and intercedes for those who wronged him.
"I have heard of You by the hearing of the ear, But now my eye sees You. Therefore I abhor myself, And repent in dust and ashes."

God's Answer from the Whirlwind (Job 38-41)

🌍

Creation

Job 38:4

God asks Job where he was when the foundations of the earth were laid, affirming God's unfathomable wisdom and power.

✨

Cosmos

Job 38:7

A majestic view of the cosmos, when the morning stars sang together and angels rejoiced at God's handiwork.

🐉

Creatures

Job 40-41

Behemoth and Leviathan reveal God's absolute mastery over the mightiest beasts, symbolizing His ultimate victory over evil.

☦ Coptic Orthodox Note: God’s answer is an encounter, not a mere explanation. The answer to human suffering is found in the intimate revelation of God Himself.
